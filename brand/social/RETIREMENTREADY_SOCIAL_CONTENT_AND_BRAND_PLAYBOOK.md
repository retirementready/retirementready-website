# RetirementReady Social Content & Brand Playbook

Status: durable working reference
Last updated: 2026-09-21

## Purpose

This file is the durable source of truth for RetirementReady social-content planning and production. Use it before reconstructing strategy from chat history.

## Locked brand rules

- Brand: RetirementReady
- Canonical tagline: **A more confident tomorrow.**
- Do not invent alternate taglines for production social assets.
- Core positioning: retirement planning should turn complexity and financial data into understandable decisions, choices, tradeoffs, and what-if answers.
- Brand voice: helpful, thoughtful, plain-English, non-judgmental, confident without pretending there is one universal answer.
- RetirementReady is software, not a financial adviser. Avoid implying individualized investment advice or a single mathematically “right” retirement choice.
- On other people's posts, contribute value without links, follow requests, or product pitches unless the poster explicitly asks what people are building/accounts to follow.
- On RR-owned Threads posts, follow CTAs may be used selectively rather than on every post.

## Content roadmap

### Series 1 — Retirement readiness / decision tradeoffs — COMPLETE

1. Retire sooner than expected / what does “ready” mean?
2. Work one more year? What do you gain and give up?
3. What would retiring two years earlier actually change?
4. What if the goal isn't to die with the biggest portfolio?
5. Retirement planning isn't about retiring as early as possible; it's about knowing when you're ready.
6. What if life doesn't go according to plan?
7. Do you know where you stand financially?

### Series 2 — Healthcare Before Medicare — NEXT

Narrative arc: **fear → options → coverage quality → actual cost → interconnected decisions → complete budgeting → informed retirement decision**

1. **“I'd retire, but what would I do for healthcare until 65?”**
   - Open with the fear itself.
   - Healthcare before Medicare is a real planning issue, but it does not automatically mean someone must keep working.

2. **The bridge after employer coverage**
   - Explain broad bridge choices, especially COBRA and ACA Marketplace coverage.
   - Losing employer coverage does not mean having no coverage until Medicare.

3. **“But is Marketplace insurance actually good insurance?”**
   - Address concern that ACA Marketplace coverage is inherently inferior.
   - Marketplace plans are regulated comprehensive coverage; plan, network, and cost details still matter.

4. **What will ACA coverage actually cost me?**
   - Premiums, premium tax credits, and household income/MAGI.
   - Sticker price is not necessarily what the household ultimately pays.

5. **The surprising connection: Roth conversions ↔ healthcare costs**
   - Retirement decisions interact.
   - Roth conversions can affect MAGI and ACA subsidies before 65; later income can affect Medicare IRMAA.
   - This post intentionally bridges into the future tax/Roth series.

6. **Healthcare costs are more than the premium**
   - Model premiums plus deductibles, copays/coinsurance, prescriptions, and out-of-pocket exposure.
   - Retirement planning should consider expected total healthcare cost, not only monthly premium.

7. **Would you work three more years because of a number you've never calculated?**
   - Series closer.
   - Quantify the healthcare bridge before deciding that healthcare prevents retirement.
   - Turn undefined fear into a planning number.

### Series 3 — Taxes & Roth Conversions — PLANNED

Develop after Series 2. Preserve the RR approach: lifetime/multi-year planning rather than “pay the least tax this year.” Likely themes include tax diversification, conversion timing, partial vs all-or-nothing conversions, RMDs, survivor tax effects, ACA/IRMAA interactions, and why tax decisions should not be modeled in isolation.

### Series 4 — Social Security — PLANNED

Develop after Series 3. Emphasize that there is no universal magic claiming age. Coordinate claiming age with retirement timing, portfolio withdrawals, longevity, spouse/survivor considerations, taxes, and other income.

## Channel strategy

### Facebook / Instagram
- Primary cadence: polished branded anchor content.
- Use a strong photograph + concise designed headline + RR branding.
- Longer caption may carry the explanation.
- Maintain visual continuity across a series.

### Threads
- Daily original content is appropriate while building the account.
- M/W/F can carry substantial anchor/image posts; other days can use short observations, questions, or conversation starters.
- Thoughtful replies to relevant accounts are a major discovery channel.
- Replies should add one useful idea rather than demonstrate everything RR knows.
- Do not ask for follows in replies to other people's conversations.
- On RR-owned posts, use a follow CTA selectively (roughly every 3–4 original posts while testing conversion).
- Topics/community labels can aid discovery when genuinely relevant.
- Polls: hold until there is enough audience/engagement for useful response volume; keep a bank of strong questions that can later become polls.

## Authentic-photo social asset production playbook

### Objective

Create polished RR social graphics from authentic user-supplied photographs while preserving the photograph itself and avoiding generative reinterpretation of the source image.

### Required sequence

1. **Start with an authentic photograph supplied/owned by RR.**
   - Prefer travel, road, landscape, architecture, or everyday-life photography with clear negative space for copy.
   - Do not ask an image generator to recreate, repaint, extend, restyle, or “improve” the photograph for the production version.

2. **Lock the copy before layout.**
   - Finalize the post's headline/subhead first.
   - Keep on-image copy concise; detailed explanation belongs in the caption.
   - Use the canonical tagline only: “A more confident tomorrow.”

3. **Use deterministic composition for production.**
   - Preserve the source photo pixels except ordinary deterministic crop/resize/exposure adjustments if needed.
   - Add typography, gradients/contrast overlays, RR logo, tagline, and PLAN | EXPLORE | LIVE treatment as separate graphic layers using normal image/layout tooling.
   - Use approved logo assets from this repository; do not have generative AI redraw the RR logo or typography.

4. **Do not generatively edit the finished production asset.**
   - If a concept mockup is generated, treat it only as a visual reference.
   - Rebuild the accepted design deterministically on the authentic source photograph for the final social asset.

5. **Quality-control before publishing.**
   - Verify exact copy/spelling.
   - Verify canonical logo and tagline.
   - Check crop/readability at phone size.
   - Ensure overlays do not obscure the important photographic subject.
   - Retain the original photograph and final composed asset separately.

### Important platform-label note

This workflow is intended to preserve authentic photography and accurately distinguish ordinary graphic design/compositing from generative alteration. It is **not a guarantee about any platform's automated AI-content label**; Meta/other platforms control their own detection and labeling policies. Never remove or falsify provenance metadata to evade a platform label.

## Photography guidance — Series 2 Healthcare Before Medicare

Use authentic photographs that imply uncertainty, bridges, transitions, choices, or planning without relying on generic medical-stock imagery. Avoid doctors with clipboards, hospital corridors, stethoscopes, senior couples staring at sunsets, and other retirement/healthcare clichés.

1. **Healthcare fear / “until 65?”**
   - A road disappearing around a bend; trail approaching an uncertain turn; broad landscape with a visible destination but unclear route.
   - Leave strong negative space for the opening question.

2. **Bridge after employer coverage**
   - Literal bridge, causeway, stepping stones, walkway between two areas, ferry crossing, or road spanning a gap.
   - Best if the visual naturally reads as “getting from here to there.”

3. **Is Marketplace coverage good insurance?**
   - Multiple paths/signposts/doors/windows or a marketplace/street scene with several choices.
   - Emphasize choice/evaluation rather than medical treatment.

4. **What will coverage cost?**
   - Travel-planning scene, menu/price-board context, ticket window, map + notebook, or landscape with measurable distance markers.
   - Avoid photographing private financial/medical information.

5. **Roth conversions ↔ healthcare costs**
   - Intersecting roads, junction, crossing paths, interconnected bridges, rail switches, or two streams meeting.
   - Visual metaphor: one decision changes another.

6. **Healthcare costs are more than the premium**
   - Layered landscape, multiple trail markers, several objects/items in a travel kit, or iceberg-like visible/hidden composition from an authentic scene.
   - Visual metaphor: one visible cost is not the whole picture.

7. **Would you work three more years because of a number you've never calculated?**
   - Strong road/path toward a horizon, milestone marker, clock/tower, calendar-like architectural repetition, or destination visible in the distance.
   - Should feel reflective and decision-oriented, not fearful.

### Photo selection rules

- Prefer vertical or square-friendly source photos with enough resolution for 4:5 social crops.
- Look for uncluttered areas in sky, water, road, wall, or landscape where headline text can sit.
- Avoid photos where important subjects sit at every edge; allow crop flexibility.
- A coherent series does not require all photos to look identical. Consistency should come from RR typography, overlays, logo treatment, and voice.
- Authentic travel photography is a brand advantage: it feels personal and distinctive compared with generic financial-services stock imagery.

## Production checklist for every new FB/IG series

1. Confirm series theme and narrative arc in this file.
2. Draft/finalize Post 1 caption and on-image copy.
3. Select authentic photo for Post 1.
4. Create deterministic branded graphic.
5. Owner review/approval.
6. Repeat one post at a time; do not mass-generate all graphics before the visual direction is accepted.
7. Add final copy/asset references to the roadmap or a series-specific section so the next thread does not need to reconstruct them.
